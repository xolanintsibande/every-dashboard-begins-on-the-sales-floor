
# Every Dashboard Begins on the Sales Floor

How five years in FMCG retail changed the way I think about business, data and performance

Before Power BI, I learned analytics on the sales floor with a pen, a planogram and 285+ SKUs.

For five years in FMCG retail, I learned that availability, promotions, planograms, customer service and daily execution all leave data behind.

When digital reporting introduced me to dashboards, I became curious about what sat behind the numbers.

Where did the number come from?
What had happened on the shelf?
And if the number looked wrong, what was actually happening in the store?

“I didn't plan to build a career in retail. Retail taught me how businesses operate. More importantly, it taught me that every dashboard begins with someone doing the work.”

# Retail Became My Classroom

Retail gave me a practical understanding of how business performance is created.
I learned through daily execution: 

checking availability, following planograms, implementing promotions, supporting visibility and responding to operational issues.

These activities can look small individually. Together, they create measurable business outcomes.
The sales floor became my first source of business context.

# My First Lesson Was About People

Retail performance is delivered through people.

Customers, colleagues and operational teams all influence what happens between a plan and the final result.
That experience taught me that business problems rarely exist only inside a spreadsheet.

Understanding people, processes and context matters.

Excellence Is Built One Decision at a Time
Retail also taught me that operational excellence is built through repeated decisions:

Checking another shelf.
Following the right planogram.
Confirming execution.
Correcting an issue.
Asking a better question.

Individually, these actions can look small. A busy retail floor can hide their impact.

# When the Dashboard Made Me Curious

Digital reporting introduced a different way of looking at the retail operation.
Instead of only seeing what was happening on the shelf, I could see measures, trends and exceptions.

That made me curious about where the numbers came from and how operational activity became measurable information.
I did not have all the answers.
But I learned to investigate.

# A Small Reporting Issue Changed the Decision

A merchandising report showed that a product was unavailable at a store.
A physical shelf check showed that the product was present.

That created a question.

Was the problem actually stock?

I checked the shelf, investigated the placement and compared the physical situation with the reported information.
The information was corrected.

The important lesson was not simply that a report can contain an error.

It was that a report can identify an exception without explaining its cause.
A number can identify an exception, but it does not always identify the cause.
That experience changed the way I approached data.

Instead of asking only:
What happened?
I started asking:
Why did it happen?
And then:
What should happen next?

# FLOOR → DATA → DECISION → IMPROVE

FLOOR
Where the work happens.
DATA
Where the work becomes measurable.
DECISION
Where evidence meets business context.
IMPROVE
Where the decision changes what happens next

This became the connection between my retail experience and analytical development.

# From Retail Performance to Analytical Thinking

The numbers on the retail floor are not disconnected from the operation.

They represent activity, customer behaviour, execution, availability, sales and other business processes.

My analytical development pushed me to look beyond the number.
What was driving it?
What had changed?
Was the result expected?
What additional evidence was needed?
What decision could the information support?

Those questions moved me from simply observing performance toward analytical thinking.

# What Retail Taught Me About Data

My retail experience shaped four principles:

Execute consistently.
Question the assumption.
Use context.
Measure the change.

Data is useful when it helps us understand what happened, why it happened and what action may follow.

# Connecting the Sales Floor to the Data

The more I learned about analytics, the more I realised that my retail experience was not separate from the journey.

It was part of it.

Retail gave me operational context.
Data and analytics gave me another way to examine that context.

Together, they helped me understand the relationship between what happens on the floor and what eventually appears in a report.

# Final Reflection

I did not enter retail planning to build a career around analytics.

But five years in retail changed how I understand business performance.

The shelf comes before the report.
The activity comes before the measure.
The question comes before the insight.
And the insight should eventually lead to a decision.

Every dashboard begins on the sales floor.
